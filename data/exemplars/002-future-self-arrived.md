Your eyes are closed. You can hear my voice. Wherever you're sitting, you can feel the weight of yourself in the chair, and the air of the room on your skin. Let that be the last ordinary thing you notice for a while.

Because the year is over now. The whole long stretch of it — the part you're standing at the start of in real life — it's already behind you. You're on the far side.

It's a morning. An unremarkable one. The kind that doesn't announce itself.

And the thing you kept not starting is done.

Not being celebrated. No one is clapping. It's just finished, the way a wall is finished once it's painted, the way a debt is finished once it's paid. A fact now, not a hope.

Let yourself be wherever a year-from-now morning would put you. A kitchen, a desk, a window with light coming through it. Don't build it on purpose. Just let it be there, the way a place is simply there when you wake up inside it.

The light is doing what morning light does — lying across the surfaces, slow, in no hurry.

And your eye goes to it. The proof. The finished thing itself, or the small evidence of it — the closed file, the object on the shelf, the thing in your hands. You don't have to decide what it is. Your attention already knows where to land.

Look at it for a moment. Let it be real.

Now notice your own body in this morning. The way it's standing, or sitting. There's a particular ease in it. Not excitement. Something quieter and more settled than that.

Because here's what's actually different. It isn't the room, and it isn't even the finished thing. It's the absence of a weight you'd carried so long you'd stopped feeling it as separate from yourself.

The not-yet. The unstarted thing, sitting on your chest every morning. The low hum of *I still haven't.* It's gone. And in the place where it lived, there's just — room. Quiet. Breath that goes all the way down without snagging on anything.

Feel that space where the weight used to be.

You can remember the version of you who hadn't started. The one finding reasons. The one for whom this morning was still a someday, a maybe, a thing other people managed.

You don't look back at them with contempt. You look back the way you'd look at someone standing in the dark who doesn't yet know the light switch is right there beside them. They weren't lazy. They weren't broken. They just didn't know yet that it was reachable.

From here, you know. You're the proof.

And you know something else from here, something you can't quite believe from where you actually sit today: it wasn't one heroic burst. There was no single transforming morning. It was a pile of ordinary days, most of them unremarkable, many of them reluctant. You did it in small, unglamorous pieces, and the pieces became this.

You can feel that in how steady the ground is under you right now. This wasn't luck. Nobody handed it to you. It has the particular solidity of a thing you earned in increments.

Notice there's no urge, in this morning, to start anything. That's the strangest part to feel. The restlessness that drove you — the wanting, the *I should be doing more* — has gone quiet. Not because you've given up. Because the thing it was reaching for is here. Behind you. Done.

So there's just this morning. The light. The proof on the shelf. The space in your chest. And a body that finally believes it's the kind of body that finishes things.

Stay here a moment. Let it be true. You don't have to earn the right to feel it — you already did, across all those ordinary days.

Now, slowly, let the morning begin to soften. The light loosening. The room letting go of its edges.

You're coming back to the chair you're actually sitting in, the real air on your skin, the day you're actually in — the one where this is still ahead of you.

But take one thing back across with you. Not the finished thing itself — that's not real yet. The other thing. The fact you stood inside for a minute: that you are, underneath, someone who finishes. That the weight can come off. That the far side exists, and there's a version of you already standing on it, waiting for you to arrive the ordinary way — one unremarkable day at a time.

Carry that.

And when you're ready, with no hurry at all, let your eyes open.
