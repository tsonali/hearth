Your eyes are closed. You can hear my voice. And the person you love is here, beside you.

It's late. The rest of the world has gone quiet and left the two of you in it.

They're close. Close enough that you feel the warmth coming off their skin before anything touches.

You're not performing. There's no version of yourself propped up for them to see. You set it down at the door, and you didn't even notice doing it.

Their attention is on you. All of it. Not half-listening, not waiting for their turn — just resting on you, steady.

They do the small thing they do. The one that's only theirs. You feel the corner of your mouth answer it.

Neither of you fills the quiet. It doesn't need filling. The silence is comfortable, and you let it be long.

Their hand finds yours. The weight of it settles. Warm where your palms meet, cool air on the backs of your hands.

And it lands in your chest, plainly: you are wanted here. Not needed. Not useful. Wanted.

You let your breath drop lower, and you stay. Seen, and not bracing against it.

Now the room begins to come back, slowly, the warmth of their hand staying with you as it does.

Carry that back — the ease of setting yourself down beside someone. When you're ready, open your eyes.
