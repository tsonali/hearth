Your eyes are closed. You're here, and so are they — the person you love. You don't have to picture their face all at once. They're just beside you.

It's late, or it's quiet, or the rest of the world has simply stepped back. However that feels to you now.

They're near enough that you can feel the warmth of them before you touch — that small heat a body gives off when it's close.

You're not performing. That's the first thing you notice. There's no version of yourself you're holding up for them. You've set it down.

Their attention is on you. Not divided, not drifting. Just resting on you, the way attention rarely does.

Something they do — small, theirs — you'd know it anywhere. Let it surface on its own.

There's a quiet between you that you don't rush to fill. With most people the silence would need a job. Not this one. This one is comfortable.

You feel it land in your chest: that you're wanted here. Not needed, not useful — wanted. Chosen, with nothing asked in return.

Their hand finds yours, or doesn't — let it be whatever's true. Either way the closeness is the same.

And underneath it all, the ease. The particular rest of being with someone you don't have to manage.

Stay in it a moment more. Let yourself be seen without bracing for it.

Now let the moment begin to soften, the warmth staying with you as the room returns.

Carry the ease back — the feeling of setting yourself down beside someone. When you're ready, open your eyes.
