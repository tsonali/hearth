You're here, eyes closed, and you're wound tight. We're not going to pretend you're not. We're just going to start where you are.

The anxiety is somewhere in the body. It usually is. Chest, stomach, throat, jaw — somewhere it's gathered. Don't fix it. Just find where it's living right now.

You don't have to make it leave. It's allowed to be here. Trying to force it out is its own kind of tension — set that effort down.

Notice the breath has gone high and quick. That's what fear does to it. Don't correct it. Just let it be seen.

And now, not forcing — just let one breath go a little lower. Into the belly, if it'll go there. If it won't, that's fine too.

The thing you're anxious about isn't in this room. It's a thought about something that hasn't happened. Right here, in this actual minute, you are safe enough to breathe.

Feel the chair, the floor, whatever's holding you. It's solid. It was holding you the whole time you were too wound up to notice.

The tight place in your body — check it again. Not to fix it. Just to see if it's loosened even a little on its own, now that you've stopped fighting it.

The fear can stay if it needs to. But it can stay quieter, further off, like weather outside a window instead of inside the room.

One more low breath. The ground still under you.

You came in wound tight. You don't have to leave fixed. Just a notch looser, a notch more here.

When you're ready, let your eyes open — slowly — and take the ground with you.
