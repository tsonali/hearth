Your eyes are closed, you can hear my voice, and you're standing in the dark just off the stage. A breath away from going on.

The floor under your feet is solid. Marked tape, scuffed boards — the stage knows you're coming.

Past the edge, the lights wait. You can feel their heat from here, a wall of warmth at the threshold.

The sound of the crowd comes through the dark — not words, a pressure, a held breath made of thousands. It's for you.

Something in your chest is steady. Not calm exactly. Loaded. Ready. The years that brought you here are all standing behind this one moment.

You step out. The heat takes you full-on, and the dark past the lights goes electric.

The first sound leaves you and the room catches it. You feel it land — the wave of them rising to meet what you gave.

You're not bracing against the size of it. You're using it. The bigger it gets the more it's yours.

There's a place dead center in you that doesn't move, no matter how loud it gets out there. You stand on it.

Hold it — the command, the heat, the room in your hand.

Now let the lights soften and the roar pull back, the steadiness staying in your chest.

Carry that center back with you — the still place that held while everything around it rose. Open your eyes when you're ready.
