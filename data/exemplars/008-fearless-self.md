Your eyes are closed, you can hear my voice, and you're about to be the version of you that fear let go of.

You're in a room with people in it. A moment that would usually tighten you. Let the room be wherever that's true for you.

The first thing that's different is low in your body. Your breath sits deeper than usual, down where it doesn't normally go.

Your weight is even on both feet. You're taking up your space — not shrinking to the edge of it, not apologizing for the room you fill.

Someone's attention turns to you. The old reflex would flinch. It doesn't come. You meet it and hold it, easy.

There's a question in the air, the kind that used to spike your pulse. Your pulse stays where it is. You let a beat pass before you answer.

Your jaw is loose. Your shoulders are down off your ears. The bracing you carry everywhere isn't here.

You speak, and the words come out unhurried, because nothing is chasing them.

This is still you. Not someone else — you, with the fear set down. You can feel underneath that it was always optional.

Stand in it. The room, the eyes, the question — and you, steady through all of it.

Now let the room loosen and begin to fade, the steadiness staying low in your body.

Carry it back: the breath that sits lower, the fear you can set down. Open your eyes when you're ready.
