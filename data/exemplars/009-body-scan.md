You're here, eyes closed, and there's nowhere to be for a few minutes. We're going to move through the body slowly, and there's nothing to do but notice.

Start at the top of your head. Just bring your attention there. Nothing to change — only notice it's there.

Down to your face. The forehead, often holding without being asked. Let it be smooth. The jaw — let it hang a little loose.

Your shoulders. Feel how high they're sitting. Let them drop, even a finger's width. The chair will take what you give it.

Down the arms now, all the way to the hands. Let the hands be heavy. Let the fingers be soft.

Your chest, rising and falling on its own. You don't have to help it. It's been doing this all day.

The belly. Let it be soft — most of us hold it without noticing. Let it go.

The hips, the legs, settling into whatever's under them. Heavy. Held.

All the way down to your feet. Notice them. Let them be done for the day.

And now the whole body at once, heavier than when you started, held by the chair, the floor, the ground beneath all of it.

Nothing to fix. Nothing to be. Just a body, resting, and you here noticing it.

Stay a moment more.

When you're ready, let the attention come back to the room, and your eyes open, slowly.
