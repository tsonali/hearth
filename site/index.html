Imagination
Engine

Guided imagination, in your own voice, on your own machine.

By Sonali Maitra. I wrote God in the Machine, about AI claiming an authority over us it hasn't earned. I'm writing Unreality, about how entering unreality is one of our greatest human powers — and how the real danger isn't imagination, it's AI that won't admit it's unreal, that poses as your friend. This is the tool I built to do it the honest way.

Working v0 · closed beta · Apple Silicon

What it is

Tell it what you want to imagine.

A version of yourself a year from now, having done the hard thing. Lincoln the night before Gettysburg. Your mother as a girl, in a room you've never been in. The kitchen you'd be cooking in if you'd taken the other job. Anything you want. Anything legal.

You type that into the engine. It asks you a few warm questions — where, when, what it feels like in the body. Then it writes you a ten-minute guided session and reads it to you, in your own voice, with real silences between the paragraphs.

When the audio ends, the session ends. There's no thread to return to. No history to scroll. No notifications. You go live your day.

Why local only

Your imagination is your business.

Most AI products want to know what you're thinking. That's the business model. This one is the opposite. It exists because what you're thinking is no one else's business, including mine.

There is no server. No account. No email. No sign-up. No telemetry. No analytics. No "crash report." The whole product would come apart if any of it called home, so none of it does.

You don't have to take that on faith. You can check.

  • Open source Read the code. github.com/tsonali/imagination-engine. Every line that runs on your machine is visible. Don't trust me. Verify.
  • Wi‑Fi off Once installed, turn off your network. Open the engine. It works, end to end. If anything needed the network, it would fail at that moment. It doesn't.
  • No account There is nothing to sign up for. Nothing to leak, because nothing exists.
  • No middleman No app store. Direct download. Apple is not in the loop. You install the binary; what it does after is between you and your machine.
  • Your voice, yours You record your own voice once. The voice model trains on your recordings, on your machine. The trained model lives in your file system. It does not leave.

How it works

Three pieces, all on your laptop.

  • Language layer

    Llama 3.1 (8 billion parameters, open weights from Meta) runs locally via Apple's MLX framework. It runs the intake conversation and writes the guided-session script. No external API calls. About 4.5 GB on disk.

  • Voice layer

    F5-TTS, fine-tuned on roughly thirty minutes of your recorded voice. You record once. The model learns your voice on your machine. The trained file lives on your disk. About 1.5 GB on disk.

  • App layer

    A small FastAPI server bound to 127.0.0.1 — loopback only, never network-reachable. It serves a single HTML page. The audio streams to your browser and is discarded after it plays. Nothing is saved as a downloadable file.

Status

Working v0. Closed beta. Honest about it.

The full loop runs: intake conversation, personalized session script, audio in your own voice, played in your browser. End-to-end on a single Apple Silicon Mac, fully offline once installed.

This is not a polished consumer app yet. There's no installer. You have to be comfortable running a few Terminal commands. The voice fine-tune takes several hours on an M-series Mac. The docs are honest about what's still rough.

If you're a developer, designer, writer, or otherwise comfortable with a working v0, the code is below. If you'd rather wait for a clickable installer, this isn't ready yet. It will be.

The thesis

We always look to others for guidance. In the end, it's only ever you.

Most AI products want to be your authority. Your assistant. Your therapist. Your guide. Your guru. They want to be in the room with you, telling you what they think.

But no one else is ever in the room with you. Not really. You already know this.

So this product asks a simpler question. What if the voice you listen to is just yours? Not a stranger's. Not a brand's. Not an AI's. Yours, talking to yourself, about something you already half-know.

The engine doesn't tell you what to imagine. You tell it. It just shapes the silence around you while you do the imagining yourself.

Without the privacy, none of that works. So the privacy is everything.
